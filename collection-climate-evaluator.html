Models, sources and limits
  • Dew point and RH: Magnus form, γ = 17.62·x / (243.12 + x) with x in °C. Dew point cannot exceed air temperature, so RH is capped at 100%.
  • Preservation Index (PI): the IPI form of the Arrhenius rate fitted to cellulose triacetate (Reilly 1995, as set out by Padfield 2004): k = RH × 5.9×10¹² × exp(−90300 / (8.314·TK)), PI = 1/k years. Ratings use IPI’s bands for the Dew Point Calculator: 75 or more Good, 45 to under 75 OK, under 45 Risk. Output can differ from IPI’s own tool by a few percent (at 20 °C and 50% RH this gives about 42 years, where the IPI tool showed 44), so treat it as the same model family, not a replica. IPI’s rate is linear in RH: a power law with index 1. Where one lifetime form is needed, this tool uses CCI’s default instead, the Michalski power of 1.3.
  • Lifetime multipliers: five published models, each normalised to 1 at 20 °C and 50% RH, using the equations in CCI’s explanation of its lifetime calculator: Sebera 1994 (isoperms; he had no data for the RH effect and took lifetime as proportional to RH, a power law with index 1 — the linear form; his activation energies of 125 to 146 kJ/mol are above currently accepted values, so his isoperms exaggerate the benefit of cold storage; plotted here at 125 kJ/mol), Reilly et al. 1995 (acetate film fit), Michalski 2000 (RH power of 1.3, adjustable activation energy), Strlič et al. 2015 (paper, 119 kJ/mol) and Tétreault et al. 2023 (modern printing paper, adjustable activation energy). The headline lifetime multiplier uses CCI’s default form, the Michalski power of 1.3; earlier builds of this tool used a linear RH term. Checked against CCI’s published table of RH effects at 20 °C: the five published models agree closely between 30% and 60% RH, but diverge below 20% and above 60%, where there is little data to choose between them. Benchmark lifetimes of 1000, 300, 100 and 30 years are CCI’s material classes and are uncertain by up to a factor of three. The headline Natural ageing rating still uses IPI’s PI bands.
  • EMC: Hailwood–Horrobin sorption model with the Simpson (1998, USDA Forest Products Laboratory) coefficients for wood. Cellulosic collection materials such as paper and textile differ in absolute value, so use it as an index of dimensional stress. Mechanical and metal bands are IPI’s: mechanical OK for 5 to 12.5% EMC, otherwise Risk; metal Good under 7.0, OK to under 10.5, Risk at 10.5 and above.
  • Mould: the rating follows CCI’s time-to-mould data (Table 1 of its explanation of the mould and lifetime calculators), which is the more cautious source for museum organic materials. Outer limits for surfaces rich in protein, starch or sugar (leather, skin, parchment, sized or dirty textile, gelatin): no germination within 1000+ days at 64% RH and below; at 65% RH germination in about 600 days and visible growth in about 500; at 70% RH about 50 and 130 days; at 75% about 12 and 35; at 80% about 1.5 and 12; at 90 to 100% RH 2 to 3 days. So Risk is set from 70% RH, Watch from 65% RH, and Good below — with your safety margin widening the Watch band below 65% RH. Clean paper, textile and wood keep CCI’s 80 to 85% RH onset for visible growth (Risk from 80% RH), but the 65% RH germination floor still applies to the Watch band. Sedlbauer’s Lowest Isopleth for Mould, RHcrit = 0.02633·cosh(0.10083·(T − 30)) + c with c = 0.7153 for LIM 0 (about 75.6% RH at 20 °C), is shown for reference only: for museum organic materials it is the less cautious of the two sources and it no longer sets the rating. The CCI curves were fitted near 25 °C and are a cautious generalisation for other temperatures. CCI treats a sustained drop below 65% RH as ending the growth cycle.
  • Material profiles: presets drawn from CCI’s “Incorrect relative humidity” tables, which classify sensitivity by material and condition. Choosing a profile switches off ratings that do not apply, and resets the material class and activation energy, which you can then adjust. Leather, skin, parchment and sized or dirty textile use the CCI sensitive-surface bands: Watch from 65% RH, Risk from 70%. Clean paper, textile and wood use CCI’s 80 to 85% RH onset for visible growth, with Risk set at 80% and Watch from 65% minus your margin. Photographic gelatin adds a blocking check: Watch above 60% RH (CCI) and Risk from 75%; gelatin is protein, so the mould bands are the sensitive ones. Clean iron and copper alloys use the IPI EMC bands. Salt-contaminated or archaeological metal uses Risk from 33% RH (calcium chloride deliquesces at 33%, magnesium chloride at 35%) and Watch from 30%, the typical critical RH CCI gives for special metal collections. Plastics, rubber and foam use the 30-year class and 70 kJ/mol, inside CCI’s 60 to 80 kJ/mol range for polyurethane and resin varnish. Not included: unstable glass (CCI’s text and table disagree on which side of about 40% and 55% RH causes weeping and crizzling), lead, zinc and bismuth alloys (pollutant-dependent) and minerals such as pyrite (no figures in the sources read).
  • Logged data: columns are matched by heading keywords; timestamps are parsed as ISO, or as day/month/year when no value above 12 appears in the first place (month/day is used when one does), and are analysed as wall-clock times recorded in the file. Samples are counted at the median sampling interval; gaps longer than twice that are reported. Mould runs are continuous periods at or above 65% RH: a dip below 65% RH is forgiven for up to 3 hours, since a short dip does not dry the material, while a longer drop counts as ending the growth cycle on CCI’s rule. Each run is compared with CCI’s germination and visible-growth times at the run’s peak RH. Chemical ageing accumulates as equivalent time at 20 °C and 50% RH on CCI’s default lifetime form; the time-weighted preservation index (TWPI) is the same integration on IPI’s PI, the harmonic mean of PI over time as IPI defines it. Fluctuation is reported as the daily RH swing, with days over 10% RH counted; CCI’s proofed-fluctuation assessment needs a vulnerability class for the object, which this tool does not ask for, so treat the swing figures as an indicator. Pest bands (below 10 °C, 15 °C, 20 °C) are likewise indicators from the clothes-moth literature, not predictors.

Limits. The static ratings above have no time component; the logged analysis adds one, but it is still a summary: IPI’s Days to Mould and the VTT mould index track wetness history more exactly than the run analysis here, and anything acting on the result should first be judged against the raw series. Mould also needs a viable substrate and spores, and these curves describe the humidity condition only.
