Models, sources and limits
  • Dew point and RH: Magnus form, γ = 17.62·x / (243.12 + x) with x in °C. Dew point cannot exceed air temperature, so RH is capped at 100%.
  • Preservation Index (PI): the IPI form of the Arrhenius rate fitted to cellulose triacetate (Reilly 1995, as set out by Padfield 2004): k = RH × 5.9×10¹² × exp(−90300 / (8.314·TK)), PI = 1/k years. Ratings use IPI’s bands for the Dew Point Calculator: 75 or more Good, 45 to under 75 OK, under 45 Risk. Output can differ from IPI’s own tool by a few percent (at 20 °C and 50% RH this gives about 42 years, where the IPI tool showed 44), so treat it as the same model family, not a replica.
  • Lifetime multipliers: four published models, each normalised to 1 at 20 °C and 50% RH, using the equations in CCI’s explanation of its lifetime calculator: Reilly et al. 1995 (acetate film fit), Michalski 2000 (RH power of 1.3, adjustable activation energy), Strlič et al. 2015 (paper, 119 kJ/mol) and Tétreault et al. 2023 (modern printing paper, adjustable activation energy). I checked them against CCI’s published table of RH effects at 20 °C. They agree closely from 30 to 60% RH and diverge below 20% and above 60%, where data are thin. Benchmark lifetimes of 1000, 300, 100 and 30 years are CCI’s material classes and are uncertain by up to a factor of three. The headline Natural ageing rating still uses IPI’s PI bands.
  • EMC: Hailwood–Horrobin sorption model with the Simpson (1998, USDA Forest Products Laboratory) coefficients for wood. Cellulosic collection materials such as paper and textile differ in absolute value, so use it as an index of dimensional stress. Mechanical and metal bands are IPI’s: mechanical OK for 5 to 12.5% EMC, otherwise Risk; metal Good under 7.0, OK to under 10.5, Risk at 10.5 and above.
  • Mould: two models are applied and the more cautious rating is used. (1) Sedlbauer’s Lowest Isopleth for Mould, RHcrit = 0.02633·cosh(0.10083·(T − 30)) + c, with c = 0.7153 for LIM 0 and 0.7950, 0.8250 and 0.8502 for growth isolines 1 to 3, as reproduced in a 2022 validation study. Watch applies within your safety margin below LIM 0. (2) CCI’s time to mould, interpolated on a log scale between published points for earliest germination (600 days at 65% RH, 50 at 70%, 12 at 75%, 1.5 at 80%, 0.5 at 85%, 0.2 at 90%) and visible growth (500, 130, 35, 12, 5 and 3 days at the same points, 2 days at 100%). Below 65% RH CCI expects no germination within 1000 days. CCI treats 75% RH and above as damp (Risk) and 65 to 75% as Watch. The CCI curves were fitted near 25 °C and are a cautious generalisation for other temperatures. They describe surfaces rich in protein, starch or sugar; clean cellulose needs about 80 to 85% RH.
  • Material profiles: presets drawn from CCI’s “Incorrect relative humidity” tables, which classify sensitivity by material and condition. Choosing a profile switches off ratings that do not apply, and resets the material class and activation energy, which you can then adjust. Leather, skin, parchment and sized or dirty textile use the combined mould rating above. Clean paper, textile and wood use CCI’s 80 to 85% RH onset, with Risk set at 80%. Photographic gelatin adds a blocking check: Watch above 60% RH (CCI) and Risk from 75%. Clean iron and copper alloys use the IPI EMC bands. Salt-contaminated or archaeological metal uses Risk from 33% RH (calcium chloride deliquesces at 33%, magnesium chloride at 35%) and Watch from 30%, the typical critical RH CCI gives for special metal collections. Plastics, rubber and foam use the 30-year class and 70 kJ/mol, inside CCI’s 60 to 80 kJ/mol range for polyurethane and resin varnish. Not included: unstable glass (CCI’s text and table disagree on which side of about 40% and 55% RH causes weeping and crizzling), lead, zinc and bismuth alloys (pollutant-dependent) and minerals such as pyrite (no figures in the sources read).

Limits. No rating here has a time component. IPI’s Days to Mould, the VTT mould index and TWPI all need a logged series, which is the right next step for decisions about a gallery or store. Mould also needs a viable substrate and spores, and these curves describe the humidity condition only.
